# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Coffee enthusiasts (18–35, urban Vietnamese) who go to cafes regularly — they want to review their favourite spots, follow friends and cafe pages, and read blog-style content about coffee culture. Secondary: cafe owners who want to build community and attract guests. Users open the app in-context: sitting at a cafe, browsing at home. They scan fast and want to get to content immediately.

## Product Purpose

CafeStory is a social blogging platform for the Vietnamese cafe community — part feed, part discovery map, part Medium for coffee. Users post reviews and stories, follow people and cafe pages, like/comment/share, and optionally subscribe to premium features. Success looks like a place people open out of habit — the way they open Instagram for photos, but quieter and more intentional about coffee.

## Brand Personality

Warm · Local · Trusted. Refined and simple. The feeling of a well-designed neighbourhood cafe: nothing tries too hard, nothing is flashy, but everything is considered. Intimate, not corporate. Unhurried.

## Anti-references

- **Facebook / generic social feed**: too noisy, too blue, too corporate, too many competing elements on screen at once.
- **Yelp / TripAdvisor**: utilitarian directory feel, lacks community warmth, purely transactional.
- **Generic SaaS cream/teal startup**: the AI-default warm-cream body + teal primary + eyebrow labels pattern — cliché 2024–2026, feels mass-produced.
- **Busy Instagram clone**: Instagram UX patterns are fine to borrow, but the loud reel-first, engagement-metric-everywhere energy is not the vibe — this should feel quieter and more editorial.

## Design Principles

1. **Content first, chrome last.** The feed, the story, the cafe photo — that's what the user came for. UI chrome (nav, badges, action bars) earns space; it doesn't claim it.
2. **Warmth through restraint.** Warmth is carried by palette and typography, not by decorating every surface. Less animation, less drop-shadow layering, less gradient.
3. **Familiar patterns, considered execution.** Borrow what works from Instagram (bottom/side nav, story rail, post card) but execute with more breathing room and editorial care.
4. **Community, not metrics.** Social counters (likes, shares) are present but subordinate. The identity of who posted and where matters more than the engagement number.
5. **Quiet by default, intentional motion.** Motion exists only when it helps orientation or feedback — not decoration. Transitions are short, ease-out, never bouncy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA as working baseline. Reduced-motion preference must be respected for all transitions. Color contrast checked at AA for body text (≥4.5:1) and large text (≥3:1). Dark mode is a first-class supported theme, not an afterthought.
